OriginalStake is a staked originality-challenge market for short creative text - settled by GenLayer validator consensus, not a moderator. A writer bonds GEN and submits a short piece (a tagline, a poem, a product name), which the contract embeds into its own on-chain vector index. Anyone else who thinks it's a near-copy of existing work can open a challenge by staking an equal counter-bond. A permissionless resolution step then deterministically finds the nearest prior submission via vector search, and a single consensus round compares the two texts - already stored in the contract, never fetched from outside - for substantial reuse of expression. Whoever's wrong loses their stake to the other side. Anyone can also crowdfund a bounty onto a submission to incentivize someone to challenge it, semantically search the whole corpus before submitting, and stale unresolved challenges can be permissionlessly expired so funds are never locked forever.

The problem: deciding whether new text substantially reuses someone else's expression, rather than just sharing a topic, is a judgment call no distance threshold or regex can make safely - and today that call is made by nobody, or by a single centralized moderator both sides have to trust blindly. This is deliberately not another escrow-with-a-delivery-step: there's no client or freelancer, no fixed payer role. Both sides independently put capital at risk before any judgment exists, and the evidence being judged already lives in the contract's own growing corpus.

How to use it: connect a wallet (generate one in-browser in seconds), submit a short text with a GEN bond, and anyone can preview the nearest on-chain neighbor before spending anything on a challenge. A challenger stakes an equal counter-bond; anyone can then trigger the permissionless resolution - a real multi-minute consensus round, not an instant result.

Measured results, run live on the deployed contract: two genuinely different real outcomes in one clean test run (12/12 transactions SUCCESS/Accepted, zero errors) - a near-verbatim spelling variant ("harbour" vs "harbor") was challenged and resolved SUBSTANTIALLY_SAME, and a genuinely unrelated submission resolved DISTINCT, with both bonds correctly returned. On the current v2 contract, a bounty scenario ran for real: a third account crowdfunded a 300-wei bounty on a submission, a challenger opened against a near-duplicate, and live consensus resolved SUBSTANTIALLY_SAME - the bounty paid out to the challenger on top of the forfeited bond automatically.
